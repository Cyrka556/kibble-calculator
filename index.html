On the bag as metabolisable energy — often written as kcal/100 g, or as kcal/kg (divide by ten). Typical dry kibble is 350–420; wet food is 70–120. This is the number that turns calories into grams, so it's worth getting right.

    Puppy amounts come from the NRC (2006) growth equation and the revision by Klein et al. (2019) on privately owned puppies; adult and senior amounts from resting energy requirement (70 × kg0.75) with standard life-stage and neutering factors, which line up with FEDIAF's 95–110 kcal/kg0.75. The cross-check compares this against the bag-style chart in PetMD's feeding guide. Sleep bands follow ordinary veterinary guidance (AKC, VCA, Vets4Pets). Puppy exercise follows the Royal Kennel Club's five-minutes-per-month-of-age rule — which the PDSA points out has no trial evidence behind it, so it's used here as a ceiling on lead walking rather than a target. Every equation is a starting point — individual dogs vary by a third either way. Weigh regularly, feel for ribs, and let your vet have the final word.
